2 · Positioning

Pick: A · "Your workshop. Claude Code is the crew." r1 sold the features. This sells the place: one workshop on your Mac that you own, for every project and for Home, where the agents do the work and you decide.

A · Workshop + crew · pick
Your workshop. Claude Code is the crew. Every project and your own life on one Mac: tasks, notes the agents write, schedules, reviews, and your phone when you step away.

Says who owns what in five words: the place is yours, the agents work in it. "Workshop" carries projects and life; "crew" carries many agents without saying "parallel". Short enough for the hero, the About line and a talk slide.

B · Plain workshop
A workshop on your Mac for you and your Claude Code agents. Projects, tasks, notes, schedules and reviews in one place, and your phone when you step away.

Clear and searchable, but "for you and your agents" puts them side by side, so you no longer own it. Good as the About line's shape, weak as a headline.

C · Run your days
Run your projects, and your days, with Claude Code. One place on your Mac where agents keep your backlog, write your notes and keep your schedules.

Most active, and Home gets equal weight. But "run your days" reads like a productivity app, and Claude Code users come for the code first.

What the new hero says, top to bottom

  1. Whose it is. Name and icon small; the headline is about you: "Your workshop."
  2. Who works there. "Claude Code is the crew." in the accent, the one coloured line.
  3. What is in it. Projects and your own life, then the things in the workshop: tasks, notes the agents write, schedules, reviews.
  4. The proof is Home. The window is Home, not a project: one question, "What waits for me today?", answered across three projects, the backlog, a cron and the wiki. Sessions waiting in other projects sit in its sidebar.
  5. The phone holds the one thing that needs you, so "when you step away" needs no words.

Worktrees, checkpoints and land gates leave the hero. They are why the crew can be trusted, so they move to the README, under Agents and Reviews.

Same safe zones as r1: everything that must be read sits in the left 600px, 40px off every edge.
