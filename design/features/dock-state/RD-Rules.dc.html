Where a dock row says what's happening

Today the count column swaps its number for words — “1 landing” — so the column goes ragged, the text is wider than the numbers, it pushes past the right edge, and the gate count disappears. One rule for Service, Gates and Graphs in every state.

The rule

  1. The count column holds only the row's number (sessions, gates, graphs), always, right-aligned in caption ink with tabular digits. The + keeps its own column after it.
  2. What's happening is one state: the row's highest by priority, in words, in the label area. Never in the count column.
  3. Attention (to trust, asks you) is an amber badge. Moving (landing, asking) is plain caption ink. Nothing else gets a badge or a tone.

What's still open is where the words go in the label area. Three directions follow.

Three directions

A · After the label

recommended

Your idea: the words move next to the label and the number stays where it is.

Serviceasks you2
Gates· 1 landing4+
Graphs5+

For: One line, so the pinned dock never changes height. The state reads in the same sweep as the label, and the count column is a clean edge of numbers again.

Against: Two numbers on one row (1 landing, 4 gates). They stay apart because the state's number sits with its word and the count stands alone at the edge. A future long label would crowd the state.

“·” or parentheses: “·” is the house separator (“held · git push”). Parentheses read as an aside. The A board's tweak switches between them.

B · A pip on the glyph

quietest

Numbers only: a pip on the row's glyph says something is on, and the words live in the hover and the unfolded rows.

For: Nothing moves, and the filled amber pip is the rail's own badge. Fits any width.

Against: You can't tell “landing” from “asking” without hovering or unfolding. A hollow pip is easy to miss. The row no longer says what's happening.

Phone and iPad

The rule carries over, but A's placement doesn't: a 17 pt title leaves no room beside it, and the phone row already has a subtitle line. So on the phone the state goes in the subtitle (C's shape), and the count stays on the right in caption ink. Moving: the subtitle is in secondary ink, “1 landing · into main”. Attention: the row's glyph fills amber, the phone's way of saying “needs you”, and the subtitle leads with the amber word, “1 to trust · plans and specs”. The iPad sidebar uses the same rows. See C-RD-Phone.

Recommendation

A with “·” on the Mac, the subtitle on the phone. The number never leaves its column. The dock keeps its height. The words sit where the eye already is, and the Mac and the phone both say the same words in the same inks.

Handoff

aThe count column: fixed width, right-aligned, tabular digits, caption ink. It shows only the row's number in every state. The + column follows it.
cWords: “asks you”, “N to trust”, “N asking”, “N landing”. On the Mac they follow the label after “·”. On the phone they lead the subtitle, followed by “· ‹which›” when it's a single gate or graph.
dEach row exposes its highest state (by the priority above), the state's count, and, for a count of one, the name of the gate or graph. The phone detail drops the words and carries only the number.

Boards: RD-A-Mac, RD-B-Mac, RD-C-Mac (each: 1280 Mate dark and 640 Mate light, four states), C-RD-Phone (iPhone and iPad, light and dark).
