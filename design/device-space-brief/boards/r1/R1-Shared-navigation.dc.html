One navigation model

Back always goes to where you were. A link pushes onto the stack you are in, even when its target lives in another section; the page tells you which section it belongs to, and a back chip names where you came from. Nothing replaces the whole stack any more, and nothing opens as a full-screen popup.

Frames use A's chrome; B and C apply the same stack with their own bars. Blue circles = a tap; blue numbers = order.

1 · Link inside a section → push. Wiki → wiki page, task → task, session → subagent. Plain back button; scroll kept.
2 · Jump to another section → push here too. Session → #4, task → its session. The page shows its home (“Backlog · shop”) and a back chip with the origin's name. ••• › Show in Backlog moves you there for good.
3 · Docs and wiki links open in place. A link's openDoc reaches the section's plugin (the wiki selects its page); the phone pushes the result, on iPad in the detail column. A file outside any section (a .go file on the Mac) opens as a doc page with a 680-pt measure, never over all three columns.
4 · Coming from outside. Needs you, Inbox and the request banner push with a back chip (“‹ Needs you”). A lock-screen notification opens the session on its own stack (Macs › shop › session), so Back goes to shop.
5 · Every segment keeps its own stack and scroll. Agents, Backlog, Docs, More and each plugin section remember their open item; switching never resets them.
6 · After you answer, the way on is in reach. The answered card collapses and the bottom offers the next waiting item or Back to where you came from.
7 · iPad: the list column belongs to the section. Wiki shows its tree there, Backlog its list; a jump from a session pushes in the detail column and leaves the list on the session.
8 · Long-press Back shows the whole stack (system menu). The named chip appears only when Back crosses a section; inside a section it is the plain chevron.
