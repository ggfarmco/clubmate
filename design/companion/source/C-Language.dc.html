Clubmate companion · design language

C2 · Companion adaptation

Same product, different hands. The principles and node specs carry over from the Mac; what changes is the metrics, the surfaces and the navigation; accent and tones are the same values as the Mac's Mate theme. One layout serves iPhone, iPad and iPhone Duo, driven by size classes, not by the device.

Accent decision

Mate green becomes the companion accent

On the Mac the brand stays out because the user picked their colours in Ghostty. The phone has no such theme — it follows the system — so this is the one window where the brand can live. Indigo is the system's default and says nothing about Clubmate.

  • accent = Mate green: the one primary action, selection, unread dot
  • success moves to teal so “done” never reads as the accent — and is mostly muted anyway
  • warning = amber: needs you (permission, question)
  • danger = red: failed, stuck, Reject
  • Surfaces and text levels are the system's — no Mate cream backgrounds; native first
  • Light accent darkened from #3E8A2E to #357A27 so white text on it passes 4.5:1

Carried over from the Mac

  • Colour is state. Amber only for “needs you”, red for failed, the accent for the one thing to tap. Running is not green.
  • Cards are for things that ask or change. Permission, question, plan, tool group, code. Messages and lists stay flat.
  • Names over ids; keep the end that differs. Commands keep the executable; paths truncate in the middle.
  • Permanent permission is never the default. Allow once first and filled; Always allow plain and confirmed.
  • Node specs: message, thought, plan (no strikethrough), tool group, diff, permission request, composer — same structure and ordering as the Mac renderers.
  • Status vocabulary: Waiting for you · Asking you · Running · Stopped · Done · Closed.

One layout: size classes, not breakpoints

Apple's iPhone Duo guidance: no fixed widths, breakpoints or per-screen metrics — compact width on the outer display, regular on the inner, and let NavigationSplitView decide how many columns fit. The same rule covers iPhone, iPad, Split View, Stage Manager and both Duo displays. Duo metrics: outer 5.4″, 1398×2034 px (≈466×678 pt, portrait); inner 7.6″, 1878×2670 px (≈951×669 pt, landscape when open). Pixels are Apple's; points and the ≈40 pt fold are simulator measurements.

iPhone Duo: bars on the side

  • On the outer display and the inner display in landscape the system moves the header to a vertical bar on the right edge. Top: Back; then •••. Stop stays inside ••• in one column, as on iPhone, and gets its own button in two columns, as on iPad. Only the inner display in portrait keeps horizontal bars.
  • Use the system bars (.toolbar on NavigationStack / NavigationSplitView), not a custom overlay: the glass buttons in the iPhone mockups are these system items, so the move is automatic. Icon-only items with titles; titles show in overflow.
  • In two columns only the detail column gets the vertical bar; the session list keeps its horizontal items.
  • The composer and a docked request are content, not a toolbar: they stay at the bottom of the content area, beside the bar.
  • The session title moves into the content as a two-line heading (title + state line).

iPhone Duo: the fold and poses

  • Nothing you can tap sits in the fold. Read it from reservedRegions(kind: .division); the transcript may scroll across it.
  • Book pose: list on the left, session on the right, 50/50; the request card and composer live in the right half.
  • Laptop pose: the transcript on top, the request card and composer below the fold — the same “answer in the bottom 40%” rule, now physical.
  • Same controls and order in every pose. Folding or unfolding keeps the open session and the draft.
  • A request arriving while you're in another session shows as a banner at the top of the detail column, not a modal.
